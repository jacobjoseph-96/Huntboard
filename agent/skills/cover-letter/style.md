# How a cover letter (and an application e-mail) is written

A reader sees the same letter fifty times a day: "I am writing to apply for…", "I have always loved…",
"I admire your innovation", "I am hardworking and a team player". A letter that could go to any company reads
as one that went to every company. Write the one that fits only this job.

## Five short paragraphs
| # | Paragraph | What it does | Comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hook | Names the exact job (with its reference number), the team or department if the ad names one, and the one concrete thing in this ad that made the user apply. | the ad |
| 2 | Why this company | One concrete fact about them (a product, project, technology, customer, plan), then one sentence that ties it to the user. | the ad, or the company's own site |
| 3 | Main proof | ONE project or job from the CV that matches the ad's main task: what it was, what was hard about it, what the user did, and what came of it. | the CV |
| 4 | Second proof | A different job, course or side role that covers another requirement of the ad, told as real work. | the CV |
| 5 | The close | What the ad asks for and the letter has not said yet (start date, hours, language level, relocation), then one confident sentence asking for a conversation. | the profile |

Two to four sentences each; about 200–300 words in all, never more than one page.

## Rules
- **First sentence.** Never "I am writing to apply for", "I hereby apply", "Hiermit bewerbe ich mich",
  "Con la presente", or the same thing in any other language. Start with the job and the reason.
- **No praise without a fact.** Not "world-leading", "innovative", "renowned", "dynamic", and not a rewording of
  their "About us" page. Name the thing itself.
- **No labels for the user.** Not "hardworking", "team player", "passionate", "motivated", "fast learner".
  Show the work and let the reader conclude.
- **Numbers** make a proof believable, but only the user's own: a number, a percentage, a team size or a duration
  goes in only if it is in the CV, the documents or the profile. If there is none, say in words what came of the
  work (what ran afterwards, who used it). Never estimate one.
- **A fact about the company** that you looked up must be true and checkable (the ad, their site, a press
  release). If you are not sure, use something from the ad instead.
- **Do not retell the CV.** Two proofs told properly beat a list of everything.
- **Close with confidence**: "I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can contribute to <the team>."
  Not "I hope", not "I would be grateful if you could consider me".
- **No enclosure line** ("Enclosures: CV", "Anlagen", "Allegati"): the CV travels with the letter anyway.
  Only when the user or the ad asks for one, add `"attachments_line": "<the line>"` to the letter.
- Salary only if the ad asks for it and the profile has an expectation.

## The swap test (before you render)
Put a competitor's name where the company's name stands. If the letter still reads true, it is not ready:
paragraphs 1 and 2 are too general. Rewrite them until the letter would be wrong for anyone else.

## The same, in an e-mail
An application e-mail (the letter and the CV are attached) is the letter in small: 3–5 sentences with the exact
job and why this one, one proof from the CV, what is attached, and a confident close. The same bans apply, and
the swap test too. A follow-up e-mail names the job and the date applied, adds one specific point, and asks one
clear question.
